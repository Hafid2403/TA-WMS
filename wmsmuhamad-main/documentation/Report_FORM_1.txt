Background of the Problem

​
​

In the Business Environment right now, companies right now are required to have

efficient, integrated, and well structured business processes to support operational
activities and decision-making. However, the company is currently facing several
challenges that relate to the business core flow, which is still not well organized and not
fully integrated across department
At present, some operations activities are still carried out using separate systems
or manual processes. As a result, data is stored in different platforms, leading to data
redundancy delays in information exchange, and a higher risk of human error. These
issues negatively impact coordination between departments such as warehouse and
shipping, causing the overall business process to become less effective.
Furthermore, the absence of an integrated system limits management’s ability to
monitor business performance in real time, Decision-making processes often rely on
incomplete or outdated information, which may affect the accuracy of strategic and
operational decisions. This situation reduces the company’s ability to respond quickly to
operational problems operations

B. PROBLEM ANALYSIS
1.​ Sustainability Aspect
The current business processes rely heavily on manual activities and
disconnected systems, which are not sustainable in the long term. The lack of
integration causes repeated data entry, inefficient use of resources, and difficulty
in maintaining consistent operational standards as the company grows
●​ High dependency manual processes increases the risk of human error
●​ Inefficient use of time, manpower, and operational resources
●​ Limited scalability to support future business growth
●​ Difficulty in maintaining consistent and standardized processes

2.​ Operational Aspect
Absence of an integrated ERP system reduce overall operational efficiency
and responsiveness
●​ Slow order-to-delivery cycle
●​ Delays in accessing accurate information
●​ Limited real-time monitoring of business activities
●​ Ineffective inter-department communication

C. SOLUTION SELECTION & SCENARIOS
1.​ Business Process Improvement Without System Integration
This option focuses on improving the current way of working by updating
standard operating procedures (SOPs), improving coordination between departments,
and increasing manual supervision in daily operations. The main objective is to reduce
inefficiencies by encouraging better discipline and more consistent processes, without
introducing a new system.
This approach requires relatively low cost and only small changes to the existing
setup. However, it does not solve the main problem, which is scattered and
unconnected data. Since this solution relies heavily on human involvement, it can be
difficult to maintain over time, especially as business activities and transaction volumes
continue to grow.

2.​ Implementation of Department-Based Systems
The solution suggests using separate systems for each department, such as
individual applications for sales, inventory, and finance. With dedicated systems in place,
each department can work more efficiently and reduce some manual tasks.
However, because these systems are not fully connected, data is still stored separately
across departments. This makes coordination more difficult and limits access to real-time
information. In the long run, maintaining multiple systems and handling system
integration can increase costs and reduce the effectiveness of overall business process
management.

3.​ Implementation of an Integrated ERP System
This solution proposes the use of an integrated Enterprise Resource Planning
(ERP) system that connects all core business functions within a single platform. With this
system, data can be shared in real time, workflows become more structured, and
coordination between departments improves significantly.

5

Although this option requires higher initial investment, longer implementation
time, and proper user training, it provides the most long-term benefits. The ERP system
supports business growth, improves decision-making, and helps deliver better service
quality to customers.

4.​ Solution Selection
After reviewing all the available options, the integrated ERP system is
considered the most suitable solution. This approach directly addresses the main
issues by reducing data separation, improving process integration, and supporting
long-term business sustainability. Therefore, implementing an ERP system is seen
as the best strategic choice to improve overall business performance.
