A.​ EXISTING AND PROPOSED SYSTEM
1.​ Existing Business Process
The existing warehouse management process is still handled using manual methods
and separate records. There is no integrated system to manage orders, inbound goods,
material requests, inventory updates, and stock opname.
​
When an order is received, warehouse staff manually check stock availability using
spreadsheets or written records. Because stock data is not updated in real time, the
information is often inaccurate. This can cause delays in order fulfillment.
Inbound goods from suppliers are recorded manually when goods arrive at the warehouse.
Stock updates depend on manual input, which increases the risk of errors and data
inconsistency.
Similarly, Outbound goods are also recorded manually during the picking and delivery, since
inbound and outbound data are not connected, inventory records are often different from
actual physical stock.
Stock opname is conducted by manually counting goods in the warehouse and comparing
them with recorded data. This process takes a long time and often results in discrepancies due
to previous recording errors.
Overall, the existing process causes inaccurate inventory data, slow warehouse operation, and
high dependence on manual work.
So, the system we developed aims to solve the problem of manual work that often causes data
errors and also makes the work of warehouse staff there easier.
with a neat flow system and minimal manual calculations, it can reduce these problems.

2.​ Proposed Business Process
The proposed business process applies an ERP based warehouse management system,
as shown in the proposed business process flow diagram. This system integrates warehouse
activities from inbound goods handling to outbound distribution.
The process begins when the purchase order (PO) is created and goods arrive at the
warehouse. Inbound goods are recorded in the system and proceed to the quality control (QC)
process. Warehouse staff perform quality and quantity checks to ensure compliance with
purchase orders. Goods that fail the inspection are returned to the supplier.
Approved goods are updated in the system and moved to the putaway process, where they are
stored in designated storage locations. The system then updates the inventory status as stock
available.
When the production department submits a material request, the system checks stock
availability. If stock is available, the warehouse performs the picking process and hands over
goods to the requesting department. All transactions are recorded automatically in the system.
This proposed business process reduces manual work, improves inventory accuracy, and
increases warehouse efficiency though real time data integration.

B.​Global Description of The Product
2.1​Main Functionality
​
​

The proposed product is an integrated enterprise resource planning system designed

to optimize and streamline the company’s business processes by integrating multiple
departments into a single, centralized platform. The system aims to eliminate data
fragmentation, reduce manual work, and improve operational efficiency and decision making
​
The ERP system provides real time data synchronization across departments such as
administration, warehouse, and management, ensuring accurate and consistent information
throughout the organization
​
The main functionalities of ERP system are described as follows:
●​ Order Management Module
This functionality manages the entire order lifecycle, from order creation to
goods outflows confirmation.
-​ Record and manage customer order electronically
-​ Automatically validate product availability
-​ Reduce order processing time and human error
●​ Inventory Management Module
This module provides real time visibility and control over inventory activities.
-​ Monitor stock levels of inbound and outbound goods
-​ Automatically update inventory availability after input data goods
-​ Prevent stock shortage and overstock conditions
-​ Provide accurate product availability information
●​ Warehouse Management Module
This functionality supports efficient warehouse operations

-​ Manage goods receiving and dispatch processes
-​ Track warehouse stock movement
-​ Assign task for picking goods
-​ Ensure accurate goods handling and storage
●​ Logistic and Delivery Management Module
This module handles distribution and delivery operations
●​ Reporting and Decision Support Module
This module provides analytical and reporting capabilities for management
-​ Provide inventory, sales, and delivery performance reports
-​ Generate realtime operational reports
-​ Support strategic and operational decision making
-​ Improve transparency and business control
●​ User Management and Access Control
This functionality ensures system security and proper access rights.
-​ Manage user role and permissions
-​ Restrict access based on job functions
-​ Secure sensitive business data
-​ Maintain system accountability

2.​ Constraints
From the user’s point of view, the ERP based warehouse management system
depends on accurate data input. Warehouse staff must correctly record inbound goods,
outbound goods, and stock opname activities. If data is entered incorrectly, inventory
information in the system may still become inaccurate.

The system also requires basic user training, users who are not familiar with the
computer base system may need time to adapt to the new workflow. During early
stages of implementation, this learning process may slightly affect work speed.
In addition, the system relies on system and network availability. Since warehouse
data updated in real time, system downtime or internet issues may temporarily limit
access to the system and delay transaction recording.
Lastly, the system is designed with a limited scope focused on warehouse
management only.

2.4​Use case scenarios
Use Case 1: Receive Goods Primary Actor: PIC Supporting Actor: QC
Preconditions:
●​ A purchase order exists in the system
●​ PIC is logged in
Main Flow:
1.​ PIC logs into the system
2.​ PIC navigates to the Receive Goods section

3.​ PIC enters the delivery details including vendor information and items received
4.​ System saves the goods with a status of pending inspection
5.​ System sends a notification to the QC team to begin the inspection process
Post Condition:
●​ Goods are recorded in the system with a pending QC status
Use Case 2: Inspect Incoming Goods Primary Actor: QC
Preconditions:
●​

Goods have been received and are marked as pending inspection

Main Flow:
1.​ QC logs into the system
2.​ QC opens the list of goods pending inspection and selects the relevant delivery
3.​ QC physically checks the quantity and condition of each item
4.​ QC records the inspection results in the system
5.​ QC marks each item as either accepted or rejected
Alternative Flow (Rejected):
●​

System automatically triggers the return to vendor process for rejected items

Post Condition:
●​

Goods status is updated based on the inspection outcome

Use Case 3: Accept / Reject Items Primary Actor: QC
Main Flow:
1.​ QC confirms the accepted items in the system
2.​ System adds the accepted quantity to the warehouse inventory
3.​ Stock becomes available for material requests
Alternative Flow (Rejected):
●​

QC marks the items as rejected

●​

System generates a return record for the rejected items

●​

The return process is initiated

Post Condition:
●​

Inventory is updated to reflect the accepted and rejected quantities

Use Case 4: Process Return to Vendor Primary Actor: PIC
Preconditions:
●​

Items have been rejected during QC inspection

Main Flow:
1.​ PIC reviews the list of rejected items
2.​ PIC creates a return document in the system
3.​ System updates the inventory to reflect that the items have been returned
4.​ Vendor is notified of the return
Post Condition:
●​

Rejected goods are removed from the stock record

Use Case 5: Conduct Stock Opname Primary Actor: PIC
Preconditions:
●​

PIC is logged into the system

Main Flow:
1.​ PIC opens the Stock Opname section and creates a new session
2.​ PIC physically counts the inventory on the warehouse floor
3.​ PIC enters the counted quantities into the system
4.​ System compares the physical count against the current system stock and calculates
any discrepancies
Post Condition:
●​

Stock figures are adjusted once the opname results are reviewed and approved

Use Case 6: Submit Material Request Primary Actor: Department
Preconditions:
●​

Department user is logged into the system

Main Flow:
1.​ Department navigates to the Submit Material Request section
2.​ Department fills in the requested items and the quantities needed
3.​ System saves the request with a status of pending approval
4.​ PIC receives a notification to review the incoming request
Post Condition:
●​

Request is saved in the system and is awaiting approval

Use Case 7: Approve Material Request Primary Actor: PIC
Preconditions:
●​

A material request has been submitted by a department

Main Flow:
1.​ PIC opens the request and reviews the details
2.​ PIC checks whether the requested items are available in stock
3.​ PIC approves or rejects the request based on availability and other considerations
Alternative Flow (Approved):
●​

System updates the request status to approved

●​

System triggers the material request processing flow for the PIC to fulfill

Post Condition:
●​

Request status is updated to either approved or rejected

E.​ Material Request Process

This flow handles situations where another department inside the company needs something
from the warehouse. They put in a request listing what they need and how much. That request
sits in a pending state until a Master user looks it over and decides whether to approve or
reject it. If it's approved, the system checks whether there's enough stock on hand. When

F.​ User Login and Role Management

This flow covers how someone gets into the system. When a user opens the app, they type in

in, the system figures out what role they have — a Master gets full access to everything, a

Basically , it's the front door of the whole application.

G.​Stock opname flow

This flow describes the periodic physical stock count that warehouses do to make sure what's
in the system actually matches what's on the shelves. Someone creates an opname session,
then the PIC goes through the warehouse and counts each product by hand, recording the
actual number in the system. The system then compares that against what it thinks the stock
level is and calculates any difference. If everything lines up, the opname closes out cleanly. If
there's a gap, the findings go to a Master for sign-off, and once approved, the system updates
the stock figures to reflect the real-world count.
B.​ Describe in detail the process inside the Activity Diagram and Data flow diagram
1. User Login & Role Flow
When someone opens the app, they enter their email and password. The system checks if
the account exists — wrong credentials means they try again, correct ones move them
forward. From there the system looks at what role that account has and sends them to the
right place. Masters get full access, PICs go to the receiving and request pages, and QC users
only see the inspection section. Simple as that.
2. Goods Receipt & QC Flow
When a vendor shows up with a delivery, the PIC logs it into the system and records what
came in. The QC team then physically checks each item. Anything that passes gets added to
stock right away. Anything that fails gets logged as a return and sent back to the vendor. Once
everything is checked, the system marks the delivery as either complete or still partially
outstanding depending on how much of the expected quantity actually arrived.
3. Material Request Flow
When a department needs something from the warehouse, they submit a request through
the system. That request waits until a Master reviews it and decides to approve or reject it. If
approved, the system checks whether the stock is actually available. If it is, the PIC pulls the
items and hands them over, and the stock drops automatically. If there is not enough stock,
the PIC gets notified to sort it out.

4. Stock Opname Flow
Stock opname is basically a physical stock count to make sure what is in the system
matches what is actually on the shelves. The PIC goes through the warehouse, counts
everything by hand, and enters the numbers. The system then calculates the difference. If
everything matches, the opname closes out clean. If there are gaps, the findings go to the
Master for approval, and once signed off the stock gets corrected to reflect the real numbers.

5. Stock In / Out Flow
This diagram is the big picture view of everything that moves stock up or down. Stock
goes up when a vendor delivers goods that pass QC. Stock goes down when a department
request gets fulfilled. Stock gets corrected when an opname finds a discrepancy. All three
situations update the same stock table, which is the one place the system relies on to know
how much of each product is actually in the warehouse at any given time.

D.​ ALTERNATIVE SOLUTION
Here are the alternative solutions:
1. Paper-Based System
One of the most straightforward alternatives is handling all warehouse operations manually
using physical documents. Every goods receipt, material request, stock count, and vendor
record would be written down on paper forms and stored in folders or filing cabinets. This
approach requires no technical setup and anyone can use it without training. However, as the
volume

of

transactions

grows,

finding

specific

records

becomes

increasingly

time-consuming. There is also a much higher chance of human error, lost documents, and
inconsistent data across different records. For a warehouse dealing with multiple vendors and
departments making regular requests, paper quickly becomes unmanageable and unreliable.

2. Manual Spreadsheet (Excel)

Another common alternative is using Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets to track warehouse
activity. Each sheet can represent a different aspect of operations such as stock levels,
incoming deliveries, or pending requests. It is easy to get started, most people already know
how to use it, and it costs nothing extra if the company already has Office licenses. The
problem is that spreadsheets are not built for multi-user environments. When more than one
person needs to update the same file at the same time, data conflicts and version issues start
appearing. There is also no built-in workflow, meaning there is nothing stopping someone
from skipping steps like QC approval or stock updates. As the data grows larger, spreadsheets
also become slower and harder to maintain without dedicated effort.
3. Third-Party Inventory Tools (Odoo, Fishbowl, etc.)
A more sophisticated alternative is adopting an existing off-the-shelf warehouse or inventory
management platform like Odoo, Fishbowl, or similar tools. These systems come pre-built
with features covering stock tracking, vendor management, purchase orders, and reporting
out of the box. They are generally reliable and well-tested since they have been used by many
businesses before. The downside is that these platforms are designed to serve a wide range of
industries, which means the workflows and terminology may not match exactly how this
company operates. Customizing them to fit specific needs like the QC flow, pending order
logic, or the internal product code system can be expensive and time-consuming. Licensing
costs can also be significant, especially for a company that is still relatively small and only
managing one warehouse at this stage.
